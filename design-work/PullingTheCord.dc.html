Your pull is why this becomes a stop on someone else's line.
what one pull does
1
The stop gets a mark. Not a score, not a count shown to anyone — just a mark that someone thought this was worth stopping at.
2
It enters the rotation for other riders' lines. Marked stops come up more often — this is the curation dial, and it is driven by riders.
3
The stop's sign weathers in. Frequently-pulled stops look established; new ones look freshly painted.
where your curation lives
You seed the line. Rider pulls adjust it. Neither replaces the other — which answers the "does this need UGC" worry: it needs signal, and a pull is signal without being content.
the tension to decide, not design around
A pull is a vote, and votes trend toward consensus — the exact thing that made Stumble's later years boring. Two dials worth holding: cap how much a pull can raise a stop's frequency, and let marks decay so a stop has to keep earning it. Popularity should be a current, not a ratchet.
the act
Reach up, pull. It costs one gesture and says "this one." The physical metaphor keeps it from feeling like a like button — you are asking the train to stop, not rating a post.
at zero riders
Fine — your own seeded line is the baseline and the cord just doesn't move it much. Degrades to plain curated Stumble, which is already the thing people liked.
