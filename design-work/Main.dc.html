Things to do on the train

Six low-effort activities that make the car feel inhabited. Five leave it a little different than you found it; one is only for whoever is aboard right now. The point is not the activity — it's that the next rider can tell someone was here.

The problem with a feed

Are.na and Pinterest need someone to stop, extract a thing, and re-host it with their commentary attached. The filtering is the artifact — so you browse someone's perspective of a place instead of the place.

StumbleUpon's trick was that it dropped you on the actual site. No intermediary. That's the property worth keeping.

The trace layer is the curation layer

Nobody writes a review. They tuck a note under a seat, scratch a mark by a window, leave the light on. Curation becomes a byproduct of visiting, never a separate authoring act.

A stop people leave things at is a stop worth getting off at. That signal is behavioral, not editorial — and it can't go stale, because it keeps changing.

the risk worth naming

An empty train car is worse than no train car. Every activity here has to read as intentional at zero riders — the car should feel recently left, not never visited. Each sketch notes how it degrades.
